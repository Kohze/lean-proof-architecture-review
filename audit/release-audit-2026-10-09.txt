1. CONTRIBUTION AND ORIGINALITY
Verdict: Pass
The defensible contribution is a source-driven comparison of preservation interfaces, an exploratory corpus analysis, and explanatory examples. It is not a new general mathematical method or a causal study of AI reasoning.
Reviewer recommendation: Keep this scope in the abstract, submission cover letter, and publicity.

2. MATHEMATICS AND FIDELITY TO LEAN
Verdict: Pass within stated scope
The inspected exposition is tied to pinned source declarations and proof bodies. Manual checks covered finite tape probabilities and budgets, Boolean contingency-table counts, the positive-block trace estimate, Mahler orientation/multiplicity conventions, cut-sensitive trace convergence, and marked-coordinate agreement. Five original Lean lemmas compile. The audit did not independently compile all six upstream endpoint proofs.
Reviewer recommendation: A compact verification-status note beside Table 1 would help readers distinguish source tracing from the targeted trace-cone compilation experiment.

3. EMPIRICAL AND STATISTICAL METHODS
Verdict: Pass with a substantive limitation
All 800 retained OpenAI modules and all 8,070 pinned Mathlib modules were re-extracted, reproducing 13,309 and 186,751 bodies with zero differences from the retained CSVs. A separate Decimal implementation reproduced all five joint-standardized point estimates within 2.7e-14 percentage points. The extractor is lexical; the 40 stratified inspected cases informed fixes and are not a held-out estimate of extraction accuracy. Bootstrap intervals condition on the extracted data and do not quantify extraction error or selection/confounding effects.
Reviewer recommendation: For stronger empirical claims, freeze the extractor and evaluate an independent, stratified held-out annotation set or parser-based reference, reporting boundary and feature accuracy. Treat the present intervals as exploratory.

4. AI VERSUS HUMAN INTERPRETATION AND PROOF SIMPLIFICATION
Verdict: Pass
The comparison concerns completed corpora, with Mathlib described as collaborative and not as a controlled sample of unaided human proofs. Section 6.3 presents interface availability as a workflow hypothesis and proposes matched-task evaluation. It does not establish speed, cognitive differences, or mathematical superiority. ProofOptimizer and Lean Refactor support the distinction between checked proof reduction and readable explanation; their benchmark results do not establish simplification of the six cases.
Reviewer recommendation: Retain the distinction between source length, checking cost, total development cost, and mathematical clarity. Test proposed speed differences prospectively rather than infer them from corpus frequencies.

5. CITATIONS AND ATTRIBUTION
Verdict: Pass after correction
All 47 references are cited, with no missing or unused keys. Twenty-five publisher DOI/title records match retained metadata. The two named companion papers now use the author-supplied SSRN abstract URLs, DOIs and exact dates, rather than GitHub as their bibliographic destination. They remain working papers, not publisher-verified journal articles. The source repository still has its own separate data/code citation. Recent simplification papers are identified as preprints; the unassessed QRH proposal is not used as evidence.
Reviewer recommendation: Use the SSRN identities consistently in submission metadata. SSRN page/registry retrieval was unavailable during this audit, so the record distinguishes author confirmation from independent publisher verification.

6. REPRODUCIBILITY AND VERIFICATION
Verdict: Pass
A fresh anonymous download of the public v1.6.0 source/data ZIP matched its release hash; all 1,535 manifest-listed files matched, and six verification scripts passed from the extracted package. The correction edition changes bibliography, a repeated-word typo, version metadata and audit records; it does not change corpus data, estimates, examples or formal claims. The corrected manuscript rebuilt without LaTeX warnings and passed its bibliography, literal-excerpt and PDF checks.
Reviewer recommendation: The optional upstream reuse experiment has retained successful local compilation evidence but is not rerun by the default CI job. An opt-in CI workflow would strengthen ongoing portability assurance; the present documentation already states the scope.

7. FIGURES, TYPOGRAPHY AND NOTATION
Verdict: Pass
All 35 pages of the corrected PDF were rendered and inspected through five contact sheets, with Figure 1 and the corrected bibliography additionally inspected at page scale. Figure 1 has aligned filled/hollow markers, no connectors and no point-value annotations. Its x axis and caption identify the two differences from Mathlib. Caption typography and Lean listings are consistent; the Crouzeix excerpt stays together. Safe(O,S,e,x) uses explicit mathematical arguments. No clipping, overlap or missing glyphs was found.
Reviewer recommendation: The whitespace before forced section/listing transitions is acceptable. A journal class may repaginate the manuscript and will require another render check.

8. RELEASE METADATA, LICENSING AND ARCHIVAL DURABILITY
