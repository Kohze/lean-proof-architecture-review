\section{Sources, selection, and reading method}
\label{sec:method}

The unit of comparison is a chain: an initial representation, a
preservation statement, and its downstream use. Selection was purposive.
A case had to expose all three parts and a mathematically meaningful
change of representation. We favored diversity of mathematical setting
and preservation requirement over coverage by file count.

For each case we read backwards from the selected conclusion to its
definitions and immediate hypotheses, then forwards through the
constructions that discharge those hypotheses. We ask which device is
inherited from earlier work, which concrete relation is proved in this
development, and which later obligation it resolves. The accompanying
evidence ledgers retain declaration names, line ranges, source excerpts,
and Git blob identifiers; downloaded files were checked against those
identifiers.

The discovery process was exploratory. The retained findings record
endpoint inspection followed by deeper tracing of candidate interfaces.
For example, the irrationality-exponent-of-$\pi$ and two-point-correlation
endpoints were examined but left outside the review because their
mechanisms had not been traced at comparable depth. These exclusions
reflect the available analysis. The selection supports a comparison of
the six cases, not an estimate of how frequent their techniques are
throughout the collection.

A central reading distinction is between a conditional interface and
its instantiation. An analytic theorem may assume a flux identity or
resolvent estimate; the next question is whether the endpoint constructs
that input for its stated objects. Similarly, a witness chosen
classically has a proved construction, while an extra provider hypothesis
remains an input to the theorem. We trace these dependencies directly.
The six case studies use static declaration and dependency analysis.
The original examples in Section~\ref{sec:worked} provide a separate
executable check of particular arithmetic and finite-law interfaces,
using Lean 4.34.1. Repository provenance and checking infrastructure are
documented in the data and code availability statement.

Blueprints connect informal arguments to declarations and dependencies
\citep{MassotLeanBlueprint}; LeanArchitect studies automation of that
connection \citep{ZhuEtAl2026}. Our evidence ledgers make the selected
interfaces inspectable. The exposition supplies the mathematical
equations that explain why their hypotheses are useful.
