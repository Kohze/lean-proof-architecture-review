\section{Sources, selection, and reading method}
\label{sec:method}

The source inventory is the complete Git tree of \src{openai/math} at commit
\src{fd4aeeb2ee4fc729c18d98444fed42fd0529eeeb}, inspected on 8 October 2026.
It contains 122,875 files with the \src{.lean} extension. This is an inventory
count, including supporting modules, rather than a count of independently
formalized manuscripts. The separate formalization catalogue lists 173
source records and 200 main-result bindings \citep{OAICatalog}. Its metadata
and the README's manuscript totals describe different units. Our selection
and conclusions concern the six developments below.

Selection was purposive. A case had to contain a mathematically meaningful
change of representation, an identifiable preservation statement, and a
visible use of that statement in a later argument. We favored diversity of
mathematical setting and preservation requirement over coverage by file
count. For each case we read backwards from the selected conclusion to its
definitions and immediate hypotheses, then forwards through the concrete
constructions that discharge those hypotheses. The accompanying evidence
ledgers retain declaration names, line ranges, source excerpts, and Git
blob identifiers. Downloaded files were checked against those identifiers.

The distinction between a local interface and its instantiation is central
to this reading. A conditional analytic theorem can be a useful component
even when it assumes a flux identity or a resolvent estimate. The next
question is whether the endpoint constructs that input for the stated
objects. Similarly, an existence witness chosen classically has a proved
body, whereas a theorem with an extra provider hypothesis retains that
hypothesis. We follow these distinctions rather than inferring mathematical
scope from declaration names.

The collection also provides Comparator challenge configurations
\citep{OAIComparator}. The separately inspected Comparator source at commit
\src{ca04cfc72b550331658ec314bf47685281bfd4bf} implements statement comparison,
dependent-declaration and axiom inspection, kernel replay, and an optional
external checker \citep{ComparatorSource2026}. This checker snapshot was
pinned for our infrastructure reading; the collection's installation
instructions do not specify that revision. The architectural distinction
between producing a proof and checking it has a long history
\citep{deBruijn1970,BarendregtWiedijk2005}. Work on a Lean typechecker gives
a contemporary example of examining the checking implementation itself
\citep{Carneiro2025Lean4Lean}. Semantic agreement between a formal statement
and its mathematical interpretation remains a separate reading task.

Our evidence for the six source cases consists of static declaration and
dependency analysis. Executable verification in this review concerns the
original examples in Section~\ref{sec:worked}, compiled with the collection's
specified Lean version, 4.34.1. This division records the scope of the
evidence: the source analysis explains the selected architectures, while
the small compiled examples check particular arithmetic and finite-law
interfaces independently. Library maintenance and semantic linting offer
complementary checks at another level \citep{vanDoornEbnerLewis2020}.

Blueprints can connect informal arguments to declaration names and
dependencies \citep{MassotLeanBlueprint}; LeanArchitect studies automation
of that connection \citep{ZhuEtAl2026}. Here the evidence ledger serves a
narrower purpose: making each discussed bridge inspectable. A dependency
edge alone does not explain why a hypothesis is the right one, so the
exposition supplies the relevant mathematical objects and equations.
