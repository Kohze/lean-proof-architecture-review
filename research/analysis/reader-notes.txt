ANALYSIS CASES FOR THE LEAN PROOF-ARCHITECTURE REVIEW

Method and intended use

These notes concern source at openai/math commit

downloaded and its Git blob SHA-1 matched against the complete, untruncated
Git tree. findings.json gives exact theorem names, line spans, short excerpts,

intentional sorry placeholders and are not the implementation evidence.

Two families justify inclusion. Their interest lies in concrete interfaces
between kinds of mathematics, not in the fact that their endpoints are famous.
The underlying mathematics uses familiar singular vectors, dual test families,
horizontal forms and cutoff functions. Claims of historical priority or
frequency across Lean developments would require a separate study.

CASE 1. CROUZEIX: FOUR COMPRESSED RESOLVENTS CLOSE A SHARP NORM BOUND

The endpoint and the useful internal interface

OAI.DirectCrouzeix.complete_crouzeix has target UniversalBound 2 [D1]. The
definitions in Model.lean make this concrete. For A an n-by-n complex matrix
and B_0,...,B_d m-by-m complex matrices, with n,m>0, it asserts

  ||sum_{k=0}^d A^k tensor B_k||
      <= 2 sup_{z in W(A)} ||sum_{k=0}^d z^k B_k||.

The norm is the operator 2-norm, the tensor is the literal matrix Kronecker
product, and W(A) is the set of <u,Au> for Euclidean unit vectors. In
particular, the endpoint does not retain a normality or nonempty-interior
assumption on W(A). This case is the finite-matrix polynomial development;
the arbitrary-Hilbert-space and holomorphic/rational variants are elsewhere.

The mathematically substantial interface is analytic_domain_core
(DomainCore.lean 15-117) [D2]. It accepts a scalar polynomial sequence b_k,
a continuous boundary parametrization h, and a matrix resolvent field R(t).
Its inputs expose every needed analytic fact: finite Faber expansion of
matrix polynomials, lower and upper coefficient-energy bounds, the exact
negative Fourier coefficients R_hat(-k)=b_k(A), vanishing positive Fourier
coefficients, and positivity of R+R*. Given a matrix polynomial F bounded by
one on that boundary, it concludes ||F(A)||<=2. These facts are genuine
conditional inputs at this local theorem; ExteriorCollar.lean constructs
them before the final endpoint invokes the theorem [D5].

The sharp constant is a scalar cancellation

Let T be the Euclidean continuous linear map represented by the amplified
matrix polynomial and let gamma=||T||. The zero case is immediate. For
gamma>0, exists_singular_pair supplies unit vectors x,y with

  T x=gamma y,       T* y=gamma x.

The supporting proof obtains a norm attainer from compactness of the unit
sphere, then proves the adjoint relation by showing that the squared norm
of T*y-gamma x is zero. It does not require a full singular-value
decomposition. matrixify turns x,y, indexed by Fin n times Fin m, into
rectangular matrices X,Y. This makes the amplification compatible with
ordinary matrix compression and Hilbert-Schmidt energy:

  P=X* R X,   Q=Y* R Y,   M=Y* R X,   L=X* R Y,
  a=integral ||M(t)||_HS^2.

The proof derives a>0 from the singular-vector pairing and a cross-boundary
estimate. It then obtains the two diagonal estimates

  integral ||P+P*||_HS^2 <= 4a/gamma^2,
  integral ||Q+Q*||_HS^2 <= 4a/gamma^2.

Fourier bookkeeping gives

  a <= integral ||M+L*||_HS^2.

Finally, positive_compression_blocks compresses R+R* by [X Y]. The result is
a positive 2-by-2 block matrix whose diagonal blocks are P+P*, Q+Q* and whose
off-diagonal block is M+L*. positive_block_hsSq gives the pointwise estimate

  2 ||M+L*||_HS^2 <= ||P+P*||_HS^2+||Q+Q*||_HS^2.

Integration now yields 2a<=8a/gamma^2. Since a>0, gamma^2<=4 and gamma<=2.
The final lines of analytic_domain_core display precisely this scalar
cancellation. This is a useful review passage: the complete matrix bound
comes from keeping both singular-vector relations and both ordered matrix
products, then placing their four compressions in one positive block.

Where the diagonal energies come from

singular_ordered_pairings and singular_diagonal_estimates
(PolynomialEnergy.lean 146-219) connect F*G and G*F to different diagonal
compressions. The two orders matter because matrix coefficients do not
commute. Left and right multiplication by a boundary contraction F cannot
increase the polynomial Hilbert-Schmidt energy. The cross-boundary estimate
and the upper Faber-energy estimate therefore bound every finite test-family
pairing of each diagonal coefficient sequence [D3].

The dual step is unusually transparent. weighted_coefficient_dual assumes
for every finite matrix test family C that

  |sum_k entryPair(P_k,C_k)|^2
      <= b sum_k w_k ||C_k||_HS^2,    w_k>0.

It substitutes C_k=w_k^{-1} conjugate(P_k). The project uses a bilinear
entryPair, which explains the entrywise conjugate in the witness. Both the
pairing and the weighted test energy reduce to

  s=sum_k ||P_k||_HS^2/w_k.

Thus s^2<=bs and s>=0 force s<=b. weighted_sequence_dual then applies this
finite lemma to every prefix and uses nonnegative bounded partial sums to
obtain the infinite sum. For the Faber weights w_0=1 and w_k=2 for k>0, the
result is the constant term plus one half of the positive-index tail.
That half-weight is exactly what anti_diagonal_energy needs to account for
the energy of P+P*. The transferable technique is to supply the optimizing
finite witness explicitly and postpone the infinite sum until a monotone
scalar argument suffices.

The Faber weights are proved, not inserted as axioms

faber_hilbert_coefficient_bounds (FaberEnergy.lean 77-110) explains the
weights [D4]. The boundary Faber series is a nonnegative Fourier series plus
a remainder having no nonnegative Fourier coefficients. Orthogonality gives
the exact identity

  E(Faber series)=sum ||C_k||^2+E(remainder).

The remainder is also the action of a nonnegative boundary kernel K on the
nonconstant Fourier series. Both kernel marginals are one. A generic
markov_kernel_contraction first proves the weighted squared-norm estimate
pointwise, integrates it, swaps the two integrals, and uses the other
marginal. Hence the remainder energy is at most the energy of the
nonconstant coefficients. Adding the positive Fourier part gives weight
one for the constant and two for all other coefficients. This derivation
reveals why the apparently technical normalization facts and the missing
constant term are indispensable to the sharp matrix estimate.

Closing the geometry-to-analysis boundary

exterior_collar_bound (ExteriorCollar.lean 76-96) supplies the abstract core
with an actual exterior conformal parametrization [D5]. Its
faber_resolvent_data defines a concrete inverse generating function, proves
its Fourier coefficients and absence of positive frequencies, and obtains
positivity of Q+Q* from geometric supporting halfplanes. Thus the positive
resolvent needed by the block argument is an output at this stage.

The final endpoint handles possible degeneracy of W(A) by a separate outer
approximation [D6]. exists_exponential_outer works for every compact,
nonempty convex K. It chooses a finite direction net containing the four
coordinate axes and builds a finite sum of exponentials of affine support
inequalities. The axes give boundedness of the sublevel; the finer direction
net keeps the sublevel in an arbitrarily small neighborhood of K. The
endpoint takes M to be the boundary supremum, sets rho=M+epsilon/2, puts
W(A) in the open set {z:||F(z)||<rho}, and chooses the outer sublevel inside
that open set. The scaled analytic estimate gives ||F(A)||<=2M+epsilon;
le_of_forall_pos_le_add removes epsilon. Smooth analytic boundaries are
therefore auxiliary objects, rather than additional endpoint assumptions.

Assessment and comparison

The proof devices above are established mathematics. What warrants a case
study is their exact composition: tensor vectors become rectangular
compressions, ordered polynomial products produce two dual estimates,
Fourier constant terms fix the coefficients, and positivity reduces the
answer to one scalar cancellation. A standard comparison point is the
Crouzeix-Palencia 2017 proof, which obtains complete constant 1+sqrt(2) and
centers the Cauchy transform of conjugate holomorphic functions
(https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.00668). This contrast does not establish that all
other approaches have that architecture or that the project was the first
to use these devices. Similarly, the finite Faber expansion itself is not
a novel basis construction: DomainCore.lean 119-151 adapts Mathlib's
Polynomial.Sequence.span_degreeLT to matrix coefficients, using unit
leading coefficients. The correct object of attention is the proof's
interfaces and exact constants.

CASE 2. MAHLER: ORDERED FORMS, TANGENT VANISHING AND BOUNDARY LOCALITY

The endpoint and the actual analytic hypotheses

OAI.SymmetricMahler.symmetric_mahler (MainTheorem.lean 12-22) concludes

  4^n/n! <= volume(K) volume(K^polar)

for n>=1 and K compact, convex, origin-symmetric, with nonempty interior
[M6]. The ambient space is Fin n -> R with ordinary Lebesgue volume.
coordinatePolar is the literal set {p: forall v in K, sum_i p_i v_i<=1}.
This case concerns that inequality endpoint; its equality classification
is a separate development.

The analytic machinery is organized around MassHypotheses [M5]. It records
positive dimension and degree, an open domain containing zero,
holomorphic components f_j, an isolated common zero at zero, a homogeneous
degree-m polynomial leading map G with no nonzero common zero, an actual
Euclidean O(||z||^(m+1)) Taylor remainder, and compact sublevels contained
in the domain. It contains no mass inequality or flux-value field.
euclideanSpecial_massHypotheses proves this package for the finite-strip
special map using an injective measurement matrix A and m>=2. Its components
have the concrete form inverseF(row_j(A,z))^m, and its leading polynomials
are powers of the corresponding linear forms. The Taylor estimate is
transported across the PiLp continuous linear equivalence in both domain
and codomain; this is why the exact Euclidean realization matters.

The generic flux result does retain MassHypotheses as inputs. The body
reduction symmetric_mahler_from_special_homogeneous_values temporarily
retains the special-family numerical homogeneous flux value as an input.
The last proof in MainTheorem constructs the special MassHypotheses and
applies homogeneousSphereFlux_value to supply that input. Neither the
numerical value nor a general Sard theorem remains in the final body
statement: positive-level noncriticality is proved for the actual special
family by euclideanSpecial_positive_regular_nonzero.

The local algebra and why the horizontal term vanishes

Write n=k+2. The deformation uses a one-form alpha_t and a one-form beta.
The continuous transgression primitive has degree 2(k+1) and is the ordered
form

  eta_t=alpha_t wedge beta wedge (d alpha_t)^k.

The ambient exterior derivative identity keeps both terms:

  d eta_t=beta wedge (d alpha_t)^(k+1)-singleResidual_t.

PowerTransgression.lean derives this from actual Frechet differentiation,
the exterior product rule and d^2 alpha_t=0; it does not stipulate a formal
symbolic differential calculus [M2]. The full residual has k+1 placements
of d beta, and HomogeneousVariationCoefficient.lean proves their exact
multiplicity. Consequently, after restriction to sphere tangent tuples,

  residual_t=-(k+1) d eta_t.

The disappearance of the first ambient term deserves explanation. It is
not a discarded premise. At z!=0, the sphere tangent space has real
dimension 2n-1. Its circle tangent i*z is nonzero. Homogeneity shows that
beta and d alpha_t annihilate that tangent direction, so their top-degree
wedge is horizontal and vanishes [M1].

horizontal_top_eq_zero makes that familiar exterior-algebra fact explicit.
Given an independent top-degree tuple, it forms the associated basis,
chooses a nonzero coordinate of the horizontal vector V, replaces that
slot by V, and expands V in the basis. Alternation kills all terms except
the chosen one. Since the chosen coefficient is nonzero and the replaced
evaluation is zero by horizontality, the original evaluation is zero.
Dependent tuples already vanish by alternation. SphereForms.lean then
instantiates this generic argument with V=i*z and the verified tangent
dimension. This supplies a concrete algebra-to-geometry bridge beneath
the transgression identity.

Two useful representations, one exact reconciliation

The project deliberately has two constructions of eta_t [M3]. One builds
the literal unbundled shuffle form and bundles it continuously using a
finite basis expansion; this gives a convenient evaluation-by-evaluation
regularity proof. The other builds a continuous product from pairExterior
and continuous wedge powers; this is convenient for actual derivative
product rules. Their recursive ordered slot types differ syntactically.

WedgePowerSlots k is recursively Fin 2 plus WedgePowerSlots(k-1). This keeps
the two slots of every exterior factor visible. sourcePrimitiveSlots and
primitiveFinEquiv both flatten alpha, beta and those ordered pairs to
Fin(2(k+1)), but take different routes through equivalences. The lemma
sourcePrimitiveSlots_eq_primitiveFinEquiv proves equality of the routes by
cases on the sum slots. The next lemma uses injectivity of
ContinuousAlternatingMap.toAlternatingMap and the two shuffle descriptions
to identify the entire continuous fields.

This equality is a mathematically significant interface theorem. Pointwise
agreement of some evaluations would not by itself identify their Frechet
derivatives as continuous-map-valued fields. Equality of the fields does.
homogeneous_flux_endpoints rewrites the primitive in the proved tangent
transgression, then feeds it to the analytic theorem that expects the
other representation. The conditional theorem is explicitly named
homogeneousFlux_endpoints_of_transgression and visibly retains the local
formula. The representation bridge is the step that discharges it.

Boundary locality avoids imposing regularity at the singular origin

The primitive is C2 on the punctured space, which suffices for the sphere
flux argument [M4]. ClosedBoundaryStokes.lean provides a reusable locality
theorem. If omega is C2 on an open U containing a closed set K, smooth
separation gives chi equal to one near K and zero near U complement.
The field eta=chi*omega is globally C2: inside U use the product rule;
outside U it is eventually the zero field. Near K it is eventually omega.

For K the sphere, that neighborhood equality preserves d omega there:
the proof unfolds extDeriv and uses the eventual-equality rule for the
Frechet derivative. Coordinate Stokes is now applicable to global eta;
d^2 eta=0 makes the integral of d eta over the sphere zero. Transport and
tangent-density congruence allow the result to be used for the original
complex form and even for another field agreeing with c*d eta only on
tangent tuples. No regularity at the center is introduced.

This makes a clean chain. The residual agrees tangentially with
-(k+1)d eta_t, so its flux is zero. The fundamental theorem of calculus
makes the deformation's homogeneous path flux constant. Comparing its
two endpoints gives the numerical value (pi*m)^n. A small-sphere limit and
the special finite-strip mass reduction feed that value into the final
convex-body inequality. The one-complex-dimensional case is handled
separately by the circle calculation, as homogeneousSphereFlux_value
shows explicitly.

Assessment and the Mathlib boundary

The cutoff principle, top-degree horizontality and transgression argument
are standard mathematical tools. Their formal integration is the useful
case: recursive slot bookkeeping supports exact wedge multiplicities;
continuous bundling supports real Frechet differentiation; equality of
those representations makes the local identity usable by Stokes; and
neighborhood equality isolates the regularity actually needed.

Mathlib at the pinned dependency revision already represents normed-space
differential forms as functions to continuous alternating maps, defines
extDeriv by alternatizing the actual Frechet derivative, and proves d^2=0
under smoothness. It also supplies Bochner-integral divergence machinery,
explicitly imported in Mahler.ChartStokes. The project builds the ordered
wedge, tangent, coordinate-sphere and transgression interfaces above that
foundation. Thus a review should not attribute exterior differentiation
or d^2=0 to this project, nor claim a first general Stokes formalization.
The particular reconciliation and locality interfaces are enough to make
the case interesting.
