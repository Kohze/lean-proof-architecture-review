Suggested organizing point

The productive distinction is between a specification, a construction satisfying it,
and the transport of the constructed object's properties to the endpoint's actual
representation. The two cases make that distinction mathematically visible. The
trace example reconstructs actual closed ideals from extended traces and uses a
topological characterization to transport them. The character-variety example
preserves a simultaneous-splitting witness with named quotient data while moving
between rank-dependent coordinates, then proves all local seam families exhaust
the new diagram. Neither record/typeclass packaging nor classical choice should
be described as an innovation in itself. The associated mathematical payload and
how the endpoint reuses it are the case-specific findings.

Trace ideal transport: formulas and order of explanation

Use T(A) for the actual extended lower-semicontinuous traces. Their values lie in
[0,infinity]; no finite-domain density restriction is built into ExtendedTrace.
For a closed two-sided star ideal I, define iota_I(a)=0 when a is in I, infinity
otherwise, for a positive. The code constructs iota_I and proves the trace laws.
Its additivity is not automatic syntax: it uses heredity of closed ideals to show
a+b in I iff a in I and b in I for positive a,b. Lower semicontinuity follows from
closedness of I via the sublevel sets. The proof also checks traciality using
x* x in I iff x in I.

An additive idempotent tau has only zero/infinity values. Lower semicontinuity
then extends zero values from finite positive approximants to the closed ideal.
This yields the inverse laws of ClosedIdeal A equivalent to IdempotentTrace A.
The order is read from addition:

    I <= J  iff  iota_I + iota_J = iota_I.

Thus additive equivalence alone already recovers the ideal order isomorphism.
The stronger TraceConeEquiv interface is needed to identify which ideals attach
to each arbitrary trace. The interface includes addition, zero, positive scalar
multiplication, and canonical convergence in each direction; the displayed ideal
transport proofs use the forward continuity field directly.

The finite ideal is

    finIdeal(tau) = closure {x : tau(x* x) < infinity}.

For a positive a, membership in it is equivalent to finiteness of every positive
cut tau((a-epsilon)_+) for epsilon>0. Canonical convergence of tau_i to sigma is

    limsup_i tau_i((a-epsilon)_+) <= sigma(a) <= liminf_i tau_i(a)

for every a and every epsilon>0. The scalar-zero limit is canonical:

    r tau -> iota_finIdeal(tau) as r -> 0+.

Do not turn that statement into pointwise convergence. If tau(a)=infinity for an
a in the closure-defined finite ideal, r tau(a)=infinity for every positive r,
but the limiting ideal weight is zero at a. The cut formulation is the essential
regularization, not an implementation ornament. In contrast, the infinity limit
is first proved pointwise, then canonical:

    r tau -> supportWeight(tau) = iota_zeroIdeal(tau) as r -> infinity,

where supportWeight(a)=0 iff tau(a)=0, infinity otherwise. Apply the cone map to
each scalar net, use preservation of scaling and canonical convergence, then
uniqueness of the limit. Injectivity of I -> iota_I converts equality of weights
into equality of literal ideals.

This endpoint is support for trace-cone classification, not spatial classification
of stabilizations. The supporting theorem is more general than the manuscript's
separable/nuclear classification setting, since those assumptions do not occur in
the endpoint type. State this only as an inspection of the selected theorem type.

Character-variety all-seams compatibility: what is transported

SameFramedFlag includes equality of the entire prefix flag and compatibility of
the named quotient maps. A statement about unframed subspaces alone would lose
part of the formal conclusion. IdentifiedBand packages a common splitting,
row/column grade isomorphisms and the corresponding prefix and named-map laws.
It is populated from a proved existence theorem. cutGeneratorWitness is opaque
with a body; it must not be confused with an unproved constant. Classical.choice
makes this a noncomputable chosen construction, with no effective extraction
claim.

The same witness supplies the shape and row/column rank equalities to the new
marked diagram, frames, punctures, and generator values. Inherited seams require
explicit coordinate equivalences and grade preservation. The local results are
assembled into eight families: selected old, inherited old, first original,
internal original, last mirror, internal mirror, positive branch, negative branch.
AllSeams proves exhaustiveness using the graftPlus equivalence and finite-index
case splits. The produced endpoint then transports that generic theorem to the
actual produced generator values. Rebasing equal-rank matrix units produces the
side-value equality; congruence through valuesFromPorts produces equality of the
generator assignment; the equality-transport lemma gives all seam equations.

The hypothesis x is an existing solution over a field. The comparator theorem
requires properness, maximal chosen adjacent rank, positive parent genus, finite
vertices, puncture data and boundary/occurrence indices. It does not require hb,
the equation identifying that chosen occurrence's boundary side with q. The
downstream producedMarkedSolution definition does require hb, because its surface
equation proof uses it. Do not silently promote the compared all-seams theorem to
an unconditional full-solution construction or to potential integral density.

Comparator and semantic alignment

Theorem targets have matching ConstantVal data, including type/universe data.
Their proof bodies may differ, as intended. The statement dependency worklist
compares non-target declarations, while targets supply replaceable proofs. The
axiom traversal follows types, bodies including opaque values, inductive/constructor
links and recursor rules. A successful correctly configured execution would check
declaration identity, permitted transitive axioms and kernel acceptance. This
statement identity is relative to the trusted challenge/imports. Mathematical
agreement between that formal challenge and the manuscript remains a different
question, addressed here by reading exact definitions and repository scope notes.

The challenge's sorry placeholders are normal specifications. The relevant issue
is the solution endpoint's transitive axiom dependencies, not the literal presence
of sorry in the challenge file. Both selected challenges allow propext, Quot.sound
and Classical.choice, and neither has definition holes. TraceIdealTransportSupport
selects 17 declarations, including construction obligations as well as transport
endpoints, and requests nanoda; all-seams selects one theorem and does not request
nanoda. Configuration is evidence of intended checking, not evidence of execution.

Audit evidence and scope

source-audit.json contains Git blob and SHA-256 identities for 93 downloaded corpus
files. The full OAI import closures of the two endpoints contain three files for
trace transport and 79 for all-seams. A lexical scan of each closure found zero
occurrences of sorry/admit/axiom/unsafe. This is a reproducible static source fact;
it is not a replacement for an elaborated dependency or kernel check. Mathlib and
other external imports are outside those closure counts. No repository-wide total
or rarity/first-ever claim is derived from these selected case studies.

Literature framing

references.json supplies 15 primary literature references. The especially relevant
architecture baseline is Commelin–Topaz (2024), including its separate discussion
of formal/informal alignment. Garillot et al. and Baanen prevent describing record
interfaces or instance parameters as novel. Barendregt–Wiedijk and Carneiro frame
independent checking; van Doorn–Ebner–Lewis distinguish kernel coherence from
additional library-quality checks. LeanArchitect's paper is relevant to dependency
documentation, but the package's presence in lakefile does not by itself prove that
the inspected case files use blueprint annotations.
