\begin{abstract}
Large formalizations can be mathematically revealing at the points where a
proof changes representation. This source-driven review studies six such
points in the OpenAI Lean mathematics collection: common-base counting,
graph switch chains, the symmetric Mahler inequality, the complete Crouzeix
bound, extended trace cones, and marked character-variety constructions.
The cases connect finite random experiments to physical tape positions,
small matrix counts to conditional expectations on graph states, ordered
exterior products to differentiable fields, matrix amplification to
rectangular compression, positive cuts to closed ideals, and named graded
maps to a constructed surface diagram. Their common feature is an explicit
preservation theorem strong enough for the next mathematical operation.
We organize these correspondences by what they preserve: exact data,
probability laws, quantitative estimates, or local differential information.
Elementary worked examples explain why weaker correspondences can fail;
the accompanying Lean examples verify the tape-index arithmetic and the
recovery of finite multiplicities from observables. Comparison with established
formalization and verified-programming literature locates the interest in
the concrete mathematical interfaces and their composition. The resulting
analysis offers a way to read substantial Lean developments as mathematical
arguments and to identify interfaces useful beyond their original endpoints.
\end{abstract}
